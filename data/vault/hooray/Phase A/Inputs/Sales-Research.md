## 1. Company Overview

**Hooray Agency** (hooray.agency) is a full-service creative + performance marketing agency specialising in hospitality, real estate, healthcare, and luxury-lifestyle brands. Headquartered at 1124 Main St., Suite A, Irvine, CA 92614 (also reported at 18261 McDurmott W, Irvine). Field presence: New York, Phoenix, Las Vegas, Hong Kong, Kansas City.

**Founded:** 1993 as "Hyperdisk." Rebranded to "Hooray Agency" in February 2019. Privately held and independent — no holding company.

**Scale:** ~22–36 employees (PitchBook: 22; ZoomInfo: ~36). Reported revenue approximately **$7.6M** (RocketReach — directional; treat with caution). Revenue per employee lands roughly $210k–$345k — above the $150k–$200k agency benchmark, implying a lean, senior-loaded team carrying significant account volume.

**Services:** Three pillars: (1) Creative & Content — graphic design, visual identity, in-house photo/video/post, copywriting, brand architecture, multimedia campaigns; (2) Data & Digital / Media — go-to-market media planning, KPI & competitive analysis, digital media strategy, social campaign creation, optimization consulting, plus proprietary platform "Catalyst"; (3) Solutions & Performance / Technology — AAA web/mobile dev, proprietary CMS, KPI analysis & performance measurement, "Personalized AI Brand Agents," automated schema, AEO/GEO/SXO search visibility.

The agency also owns **Momentum AIQ** — an AI-powered search visibility and AEO (Answer Engine Optimisation) monitoring tool, private-labelled from Meridian. Launched approximately Q1–Q2 2026. Positions Hooray as technology-forward vs. pure services shops.

**Catalyst:** Pixel-based website traffic deanonymisation tool that captures and routes visitor data into hotel CRMs (Revenate, Sendine). Hooray-managed.

## 3. Business Model & Revenue

- **Business model:** B2B agency retainer + project fees from hotel/resort clients
- **Revenue driver:** Recurring monthly retainer per hotel client for performance marketing management, reporting, and creative services. Many clients are management groups / collections with multiple properties — one "account" can equal many properties, each generating its own monthly reporting.
- **Pricing model (publicly benchmarked):**
  - Full-service multi-channel agency programs: $8,000–$25,000/month
  - Mid-market 2–3 channel programs: $4,000–$12,000/month
  - Paid media management: typically 10–20% of ad spend or flat fee, whichever is greater
  - Agencies target 75–85% utilisation (billable hours) and 50%+ gross/delivery margin
- **Talent cost benchmarks (US public data):**
  - Account managers: ~$80k/year (~$39/hr base)
  - Account directors: ~$208k/year (~$100/hr base)
  - Media/marketing analysts: ~$64k–$80k/year
  - Fully-loaded junior analyst: ~$70k–$95k/year = ~$5,800–$7,900/month
  - Billable rates: 2–3x cost — typically $125–$200+/hr (Promethean Research: $150–$224/hr)
- **Product revenue (emerging):** Momentum AIQ represents a shift toward SaaS-adjacent product revenue from the hotel client base
- **Revenue per employee:** ~$210k–$345k range (above $150k–$200k agency benchmark) — lean, senior-loaded

## 8. Pain Points (Hypothesised from Public + Discovery Signals)

1. **Reporting overhead:** Industry benchmark: 5–10 hours per client per month (AgencyAnalytics, 7,000 agencies). For ~30 hotel/property accounts: ~150–300 hours/month total. At opportunity value of $150+/hr: ~$22k–$45k/month of recoverable capacity.
2. **Brief quality degradation:** Industry confirmation: 53% of agency briefs are complete but lack focus; 27% incomplete and inconsistent (Joanne Davis Consulting / Scan International, 293 C-level respondents, reported Ad Age). The waste compounds at scale.
3. **Scaling constraint:** Growing the account base requires more headcount under the current model. The agency can't scale efficiently without solving the coordination problem.
4. **AI investment without ROI:** They built an internal chatbot; it stalled. This is a sunk-cost frustration and an internal credibility problem for the AI-forward champions (David, Roger). Industry context: un-governed builds return 0.7× ROI; eval-first builds return 11×.
5. **Competitive pressure on AI capabilities:** Other agencies + the "agentic hospitality" vendor category are encroaching. Hooray needs to show hotel clients that their agency uses AI to deliver faster, better outcomes.
6. **Data governance risk:** Client hotel data lives across mixed-ownership accounts (agency master vs. client-delegated). Bringing AI into the reporting workflow raises contract and PII questions that need a governance layer.
7. **Function transformation ceiling:** Without SAL, the account function is capped. With SAL: State 03 where the same team carries Hotels A–F+ vs. the current Hotels A–C model.

## 9. Supernal Angle

**Primary angle:** Hooray has an agency problem, not a hotel problem. No one in the market has built AI for this specific internal workflow. Supernal is building custom — bespoke to Hooray's exact data stack, brief format, Monday.com workflow, and governance requirements.

**The four things no SaaS tool does for Hooray:**
1. Trade Desk + Monday.com fusion as first-class reporting sources — no SaaS platform natively covers both
2. Hospitality-tuned narrative (RevPAR, ADR, booking pace, OTA vs. direct) — generic tools produce generic "AI voice"
3. The intake → classify → clarify → package workflow — no point-product does this end-to-end
4. Hooray-owned governance — no vendor lock-in; data stays client-controlled; no external model training on client data

**Pricing frame (Compass research):**
- **Vs. SaaS:** Those tools are dashboards and data plumbing — they don't write the report, catch the anomaly, or recommend the next step. The human hours remain. SAL removes the labor, not just the data pull.
- **Vs. analyst headcount:** A junior analyst costs $5,800–$7,900/month fully loaded, can't work nights/weekends, and adds management overhead. SAL at $5k/month is cheaper and doesn't consume management time.
- **Vs. build path:** Stalled Vertex migration has unknown sunk cost + ongoing opportunity cost (every dev hour on internal tooling is unbillable). An external build equivalent would be $75k–$250k. Supernal's custom build is a fraction of that.
- **Vs. enterprise platform (NinjaCat/Improvado):** ~$36k–$120k+/year, plus significant integration labor, still no brief intake. SAL covers more scope for less.

**The eval-first differentiator (Compass research, 250-agency 2026 survey):**
Hooray's in-house build stalled because they lacked the evaluation infrastructure to prove it worked. Supernal ships with an eval harness built in — golden-dataset comparisons, blind QA tests, human-in-the-loop sign-off, source citations. This is what separates 11× ROI from money-losing deployments. This should be a named deliverable, not a footnote.

**ROI frame (Vision Deck — to confirm with Hooray):**
- Current account team annual cost: $235K ($105K AD + $130K for 2 AEs)
- ~$141K/yr currently spent on coordination work SAL can absorb (60% of $235K)
- With SAL: 15% coordination / 85% strategy
- ~15 hrs/week reclaimed per account team member
- +30% accounts absorbable without new headcount
- $65K AE hire avoided in first growth cycle

**Industry-validated overlay:**
- ~30 accounts × 5–10 hrs/month = ~150–300 hours/month reporting burden
- At $150/hr opportunity value: ~$22k–$45k/month recoverable
- SAL at $5k/month = 11–22% of the capacity it recovers
