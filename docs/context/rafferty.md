## 4. Why they bought

**Ventures OS (Jack and Henry).**
- Context was getting lost between meetings, email and notes. Jack wanted discussions turned into tasks and follow-ups in one prioritised view (Prototype Review, 13 May 2026).
- Time. About 24 hours a week in meetings; by a later estimate 40% of a GP's week goes on sourcing and first meetings (Technical Scoping, 1 May 2026; Tauseef / Thomas (Rafferty), 3 Aug 2026).
- Manual prep. Each first call was prepared by prompting Claude by hand (Notion: Rafferty Ventures account page).
- A small team that will stay small. Their stated biggest challenge was centralising information (Notion: Opportunity Report › Meeting 4th).
- Tracking introductions and favours for portfolio companies, which they see as part of their value (Notion: Opportunity Report › Meeting 4th).
- A texting interface. They wanted to message a number at night with a thought and have it logged (Notion: Opportunity Report › Meeting 4th).
- What was shown: a working prototype built in two weeks. Jack asked for almost no changes. He said an auto-drafted reply that was 80% right would be a major time saving, and that he wanted to see what the AI did rather than trust it blindly (Prototype Review, 13 May 2026).
- The biggest ask from Jack and Henry: make the platform Jack's primary communication channel, not a tracking layer (Prototype Review, 13 May 2026).
- Terms that helped: client owns code and data, flat fee with usage absorbed, "forever build" with no maintenance mode (Next Steps, 8 Jun 2026).
- Alternatives: Affinity itself (which shipped AI agents in August), Claude Enterprise with the Affinity connector (Drew asked directly how Ventures OS differs), GP View for portfolio reporting, Visible VC as their ideal for portfolio updates (Affinity Deep Dive, 11 Aug 2026; Engineering Kickoff, 5 Aug 2026).
- Price sensitivity: Jack said it was fine as long as it was not half a million dollars a year (Raff Scoping/Order Forms/Proposal, 26 May 2026).

**CAIO advisory (Shari and Michael).**
- Rafferty was rolling out Claude Enterprise and saw it as a change in how the firm works, not a project. They wanted help choosing vendors and projects, building culture and training people (Notion: Opportunity Report › implementing across firm).
- The finance team had concrete pain: company reconciliations in Excel take Brian about 8 hours a month, and month-end close takes 10 to 15 days against a goal of one to two (Notion: Opportunity Report).
- Jack's framing: they hear startups talk about AI and compare that with their own legacy overhead (Notion: Opportunity Report › Notes).
- The CAIO seat was priced at a third of Supernal's standard $15,000 a month to win the coordinating role across the holding company (Next Steps, 8 Jun 2026).
- The investor relationship with Infinity made Supernal the default partner (inferred from Raff Scoping/Order Forms/Proposal, 26 May 2026).

**Why the later deals did not close.**
- Holdings OS: Michael saw value but doubted he would use the interactive layer; the client already paid for a tool said to do about 90% of the same thing; pricing moved several times and a conflicting quote came from Brennan; then Michael found a cheaper vendor (Life App & Overall Supernal AI Update, 20 Aug 2026; NEW GTM + Scoping, 20 and 21 Aug 2026; Sync: Katie & Tauseef, 18 Sep 2026).
- Direxion: the marketing agency is entrenched at 70% or more of production, and the contacts stalled (Raff Scoping/Order Forms/Proposal, 26 May 2026; Slack #acnt-rafferty-holdings-internal, 17 Aug 2026).
- What reopened the door at Holdings in October was not a build. It was Shari asking for training on tools the firm already owns, then asking for hiring help (Account OS).

### 6.5 Feature rationale

1. **Affinity stays the system of record; Ventures OS does not become a CRM.** Reason: the team lives in Affinity and Thomas had warned against rebuilding one. Decided at the kickoff and confirmed at the Affinity deep dive (Engineering Kickoff, 5 Aug 2026; Affinity Deep Dive, 11 Aug 2026).
2. **One AI employee, not three workflows.** Thomas cut "chief of staff, deal researcher, portfolio monitor" down to one to keep the story and the build simple (Tauseef / Thomas (Rafferty), 3 Aug 2026).
3. **Argus on Hermes with chat and memory, not a dashboard alone.** Reason: a dashboard-only product would see low use, as on earlier projects; a conversational coworker is the real difference from Affinity. Katie required it be framed as milestone extensions with cost tracked (AI Coworker for Rafferty Ventures, 27 Aug 2026).
4. **Transcript access follows the calendar invite.** If you were invited you can see the transcript; files and Affinity data stay shared. Reason: Henry's sensitive conversations differ from the day-to-day (Engineering Kickoff, 19 Aug 2026).
5. **Five stages with explicit triggers; "Holding pattern" is a flag, not a stage.** New, Screening, Diligence, Term Sheet, Portfolio, plus Closed. Reason: tasks and briefs were noise without knowing what each stage needs (Deal Structure Q & A, 25 Sep 2026).
6. **GP View dropped; build an email-based financials request instead.** Reason: GP View has no API, its emails come from a no-reply address and companies ignore them (Weekly Checkpoint, 9 Sep 2026).
7. **Suggested tasks need accept or reject with a reason.** Reason: Jack liked the tasks but wanted a way to train them, and stale ones pile up (Weekly Checkpoint, 9 and 23 Sep 2026).
8. **Email is read only for deal domains, and only highlights surface.** Reason: SMPL's concern about mailbox permissions and business email compromise. Each new kind of client data sent to a model provider is recorded as a decision (Weekly Checkpoint, 8 Oct 2026; Slack, 9 Oct and 6 Nov 2026).
9. **Do not guess which company a transcript belongs to.** Reason: the guess from nearby calendar calls was wrong 37 times out of 37 on staging. Showing "no deal" is better than wrong data (Slack #acnt-rafferty-ventures-internal, 5 Nov 2026).
10. **A Teams app rather than a channel post.** Reason: interactive questions need an app, and it keeps the conversation inside Ventures OS context (SMPL coordination, 27 Oct 2026).
11. **Holdings OS is read and report only.** It never executes transactions or writes back to banks or brokers; every source is either a live connection or an agreed export by week 4. Reason: personal financial data and no clear route to bank APIs (Drive: 002 ... › Standards; NEW GTM + Scoping, 21 Aug 2026).
12. **Builds sit outside Rafferty's Claude Enterprise account and infrastructure.** Reason: so SMPL's review of Claude Enterprise cannot hold up the builds (SMPL <> Supernal, 8 Jul 2026).

### Chapter 3. How the deal is built

### Chapter 5. Ventures OS and why it is built this way

Ventures OS does not replace Affinity, the CRM the team uses all day. It mirrors Affinity on a schedule and adds what Affinity lacks: the three partners' calendars, their deal-related email, the files in the Investments SharePoint site, and call transcripts. From that it builds a page per deal, laid out for the deal's stage, with a brief for the next call, the tasks each side owes, research on the company, and alerts when things go quiet. It also produces the monthly deck for Michael.

Several choices explain its shape. Affinity released its own AI features a week after kickoff, so a plain dashboard would have been redundant. Tauseef argued for a conversational coworker instead, and Argus was built on the Hermes framework so that it could remember, monitor and take instructions. Argus is told never to fill a gap: if a tool returns nothing, it says what it checked. The five stages and their triggers came from a long question-and-answer session with Jack, after early briefs proved to be lists of one-liners. Transcripts are visible only to people who were on the invite, because Henry's conversations can be sensitive. Email is read only for deal domains and shown as highlights, which is what SMPL would approve. GP View, the old portfolio reporting tool, was dropped because nobody answered its emails.

The latest lesson came on 4 Nov. A rule that guessed a transcript's company from nearby calendar entries was wrong every time. It was switched off. Showing "no deal" is safer than showing the wrong deal.

### Chapter 8. What value has shown, and what has not

The clearest wins are qualitative. Jack said the first generated monthly deck beat the hand-built one. He generated a report from his phone in the middle of a call. On the Holdings side the Chief AI Officer work produced little that Shari could point to for its first three months, which is why that engagement was rated deep red in September. The training programme has changed that. Its value will be easier to show: sessions held, people trained, quiz pass rates, and a planned before-and-after comparison of tool use.
