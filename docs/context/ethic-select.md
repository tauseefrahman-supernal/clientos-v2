## 4. Why they bought

Causes of the first deal:
1. **Their only developer was leaving.** One developer, a college student, was about to leave for his own startup; Supernal offered to take over the engineering function (Drive: supernal-ethic-select-v2.html).
2. **A live pilot they could not pause.** Ethic Select was about seven months into a twelve-month UC pilot with UC funding, and needed continuity (Drive: Background for Scoping).
3. **A proof that shocked the buyer.** A sandbox test showed UC's assumed sustainability compliance of 40% was 8% when verified; the proposal led with this (Drive: supernal-ethic-select-v2.html).
4. **A regulatory clock.** California SABRC enforcement was framed as arriving at the end of 2026, with 3% reported against 70% required across 100+ state agencies (Drive: proposal). Todd later found no fixed date (Notion: Sep 11 Readout).
5. **Price and terms.** One number, $8,000 a month, start the week of the handoff, client owns all code (Drive: proposal).
6. **Ownership answer.** In the first call Johnny stressed what the client would own versus what Supernal owns (Drive: Background for Scoping).
7. **Alternative.** They had talked to another AI firm before Supernal (Drive: Background for Scoping); its name is not recorded.

What unlocked the expansion conversation:
- The API alone caps the business at a data-licence size; owning a buyer surface changes the economics (Notion: Ethic Select Extension).
- About half of UC spend happens outside the procurement portals, on distributor sites, where the API cannot reach (Strat², 15 Oct 2026).
- LabViva and JAGGAER delays; the extension removes that dependency (Notion weekly report, 26–30 Oct 2026).
- Todd needs a clear product story for investors (Notion: Sep 11 Readout).
- UC said the database is the strategic asset and that SABRC must be in any commercial package (Notion weekly reports, 5–9 Oct and 2–6 Nov 2026).

## 6. Product

**Live today**
- **Certified product database.** About 3.1M products from 10 certifiers; ENERGY STAR is 94.6% (1.9M records) (Strat², 15 Oct 2026; Notion weekly report, 26–30 Oct 2026). Used by the API and dashboard. State: live.
- **Ethic Select API.** On-demand, bulk-match API with documentation and access controls, built so partners never hold the full dataset (Linear M3; Notion: Ethic Select Extension). Users: LabViva (proof of concept done, zero errors), JAGGAER (waiting). State: live, not yet in a production buying flow.
- **Dashboard / web app.** Record counts, coverage per certifier, SABRC section, natural-language questions on the data, search by product, brand or manufacturer (Strat², 15 Oct 2026). Users: Todd; shown to UC. State: live.
- **SABRC data.** 631 SABRC-compliant products from Green Seal, SCS Global and GreenCircle; UL SPOT blocked (Notion weekly report, 5–9 Oct 2026; Linear). State: partial.

**Proposed (Phase 2), prototyped**
- **Buyer extension (Chrome, and Edge for UC).** Reads a product page on any supplier or punch-out site, shows the certification answer with its source, logs the check (Linear M2). Users: UC requisitioners. State: clickable prototype.
- **Three verification agents: SABRC, Social, Environmental.** Run when the database has no answer, gather evidence, apply the rules, and write the result back so the catalog grows with use (Strat², 15 Oct 2026). State: prototype; SABRC blueprint 72%.
- **Command Center** (also called Verification Ledger and UC Campus Reports). Every check on record, determinations list, per-agent pages, campus reports for UC leadership (Linear M1; Slack, 21 Oct 2026). State: prototype.

**Design approach and component work**

This account is the squad's reference for design done by non-designers with AI tools. What was done and why:
- **A repeatable design workflow.** Jaime connected Mobbin (a paid library of screenshots from top apps, about $40 for three months) to Claude Code and Figma through MCP: brief, pick reference apps, generate wireframes, refine, final design. A full prototype took under 1.5 hours (Squad Lab, 29 Sep 2026). The reason: no designer on the squad, and the team had judged its own apps too dense (Squad Lab, 10 Sep 2026; Weekly Review, 25 Sep 2026).
- **Reference-led screens.** The Command Center dashboard, ask and search screens were modelled on Midday, Sana AI and Dropbox Dash (Strat², 12 Nov 2026).
- **A component vocabulary in the proposal deck.** The June HTML proposal uses a fixed token set (paper background, ink text, lime, teal, rose and amber accents; Inter for body, Libre Baskerville for headlines) and named components: slide rail, image cards with pills, a two-number gap comparison, status table with tags, three-box architecture flow, horizon cards, Gantt rows, retainer price box, numbered start steps (Drive: supernal-ethic-select-v2.html). No separate design-system file for the product was found.
- **Client branding applied to the platform.** Branding integration done in September; Ethic Select domain part of M3 (Weekly Review, 11 Sep 2026; Linear).
- **Prototype made to feel real.** The extension is emulated as a right-hand drawer with an icon top right, on a mock distributor page, with a Verify button that shows the three agents working and the evidence behind the result (Strat², 15 Oct 2026). For the next pitch the squad planned a "magic trick" demo using UC's own data (Weekly Review, 6 Nov 2026).
- **Video as a design artifact.** A narrated intro video for the vision was made in about 40 minutes with Claude Code, Remotion, an ElevenLabs narrator voice and generated music (Squad Lab, 6 Oct 2026).
- **Interactive commercial artifacts.** Proposal pages with a slider that trades price against delivery time, a "What's Included" block per level, and team-allocation pop-ups (Strat², 15 Oct 2026; Slack, 21 Oct 2026).
- **Diagrams with a colour code.** The "API to Buyer Surface" diagram shows what is live in green and the proposed expansion in pink (Notion: Sep 11 Readout).

**Feature rationale**
1. **AI works upstream; buyers get deterministic answers.** Enrichment runs ahead of time and the buyer sees stored, auditable results, because institutions need consistency and an audit trail. Came from UC's own steer and Todd's reframe (Drive: Background for Scoping, May 2026; proposal, Jun 2026).
2. **API-first, partners never hold the full dataset.** Protects the client's data asset when integrating with LabViva and JAGGAER (Slack, 27 Jul 2026 question from Thomas; Notion: Ethic Select Extension).
3. **Buyer surface as a browser extension, not a new procurement platform.** Reaches the half of spend outside portals and the 100+ California institutions not on LabViva or JAGGAER, and avoids building purchase orders and invoicing (Notion: Ethic Select Extension, 9 Sep 2026; client sync, 11 Sep 2026).
4. **Badge from cache, agent runs asynchronously.** A badge must appear in under a second, so the agent never blocks the page; misses show a pending state (Notion: Sep 11 Readout).
5. **"Verified only with evidence", with separate provenance.** Agent-derived records are stored apart from certifier-sourced ones because a false badge is worse than none (Notion: Sep 11 Readout).
6. **SABRC first, then Social, then Environmental.** Todd's direction; UC needs SABRC to commit (Slack, 21 Oct 2026; Notion weekly report, 19–23 Oct 2026). Earlier order was Environmental first (Strat², 15 Oct 2026).
7. **SABRC as a rules engine plus evidence agent, not a certifier feed.** No SABRC certifier exists; data is supplier-asserted (client sync, 11 Sep 2026; Linear M3).
8. **Platform-independent pilot.** Extension plus a simplified Command Center, so the pilot does not wait on LabViva or JAGGAER (strategy call, 30 Oct 2026, per Notion weekly report).
9. **Command Center reduced to a ledger and campus reports.** Simpler to explain and tied to what UC leadership reports (Slack, 21 Oct 2026).
10. **Price-versus-time slider.** Lets the client choose a lower price with slower delivery instead of negotiating scope (alignment call, 9 Oct 2026, per Strat², 15 Oct 2026).

## 13. Book

### Chapter 1: The account in one page

Ethic Select is a small company led by Todd Capps. It collects certification data from bodies such as ENERGY STAR and Green Seal and matches it to products, so that a public buyer can prove a purchase was sustainable. Its only customer is the University of California, which funds a pilot. In June 2026 Supernal signed a 12-month order form at $8,000 a month, $96,000 in total, to become the company's engineering team after its single developer left.

In five months the squad took the database from about 2 million to 3.1 million products, connected eight and then ten certifiers, shipped an API that LabViva tested without errors, and built a dashboard. Health is 3.6, in the Watch band, with executive engagement at 5 and gross margin at 2.

The account now turns on an expansion. Supernal designed a second product for Todd: a browser extension that checks a product at the moment of purchase, three agents that verify what the database does not know, and a Command Center that reports by campus. A $48,000 expansion deal is at Order Form with an expected close of 20 Nov 2026. UC agreed in principle on 5 Nov to sign a contract with Ethic Select, and Todd's investors are waiting for that contract.

Sources: Account OS; Drive: 01 Ethic Select Order Form; Linear; Notion weekly reports; Delivery Sync, 9 Nov 2026.

### Chapter 2: Why they bought

Todd came to Supernal through a referral from Chuck Stump of HMS Jets. The first call was on 27 May 2026 with his partners Brent Bottelsen and John. Several things pushed the purchase at once.

The first was a gap in the team. One developer had built the database and was leaving for his own startup. Ethic Select was seven months into a twelve-month UC pilot and could not stop. The second was proof. In a sandbox test, UC's assumed sustainability compliance of 40% turned out to be 8% when checked against certifier data. Thomas built the proposal around that number. The third was a deadline. California's recycled-content programme, SABRC, was described as moving to enforcement at the end of 2026, with agencies reporting 3% against a 70% requirement.

The offer was simple: one retainer of $8,000 a month, start in the week of the developer handoff, all code owned by the client. Johnny's point in the first call, what you own versus what we own, mattered to a founder protecting a data asset. They had spoken to another AI firm first.

The price was low for the work, and that choice shaped everything after. It created a margin problem for Supernal and an expectation in Todd that the whole roadmap was included.

Sources: Drive: Ethic Select - Background for Scoping; Drive: supernal-ethic-select-v2.html; GTM + Scoping, 6 Oct 2026.

### Chapter 3: How the deal is built

There is one signed order form, OF-001, effective 16 Jun 2026. It commits Supernal to a first phase with three milestones: developer knowledge transfer by week 2, certifiers six and seven by the end of month 1, and all eight by the end of month 2. After that the work runs in monthly cycles on a backlog that both sides reprioritise, for the same fee. The backlog lists the LabViva and JAGGAER integrations and a SABRC pipeline, marked as indicative.

Three terms matter. The client owns all code, data, infrastructure and third-party accounts. The client may exit at the end of month 6. And section 8 says new ideas beyond the order form need their own agreement. That clause is the boundary the team has held since September.

The money flows in a chain. UC pays Ethic Select for the pilot, about $150,000 so far. Ethic Select pays Supernal. Supernal builds what UC evaluates. Supernal has no contract with UC, LabViva or JAGGAER.

The second deal has had many shapes: an anchor above $15,000 a month, options of $18,750 and $26,000, tiers of $16,000, $19,000 and $23,000, six levels from $12,000 to $26,000, and now $48,000 in Account OS. Margin today is reported as 18%, 3% or below break-even, depending on the source.

Sources: Drive: 01 Ethic Select Order Form; Notion: Scope, Liability and Alignment Pack; Notion: Expansion Alignment Breakdown; Slack #acnt-ethic-select-internal, 21 Oct 2026; Account OS.

### Chapter 4: The product and why it is built this way

The live product is a data layer. Certifier feeds are ingested, normalised and matched to products by identifier. An API serves answers to procurement platforms without handing over the dataset. A dashboard shows coverage and lets Todd ask questions of the data.

The first design rule came from UC and from Todd before the deal: AI does its work ahead of time, and the buyer sees stored, auditable answers. Institutions need the same answer every time and a trail behind it.

The second product came from two facts found during delivery. About half of UC's spend happens outside the procurement portals, on supplier sites. And the platforms were slow: LabViva kept delaying its interface work and JAGGAER wanted proof of demand. An extension in the buyer's browser reaches both problems. When the database has an answer the badge appears at once. When it does not, an agent gathers evidence and writes a result back, so the catalog grows with use. The team insisted on an evidence gate and on keeping agent-derived records apart from certifier records, because a wrong badge is worse than none.

SABRC changed shape too. There is no SABRC certifier to connect to; the data is asserted by suppliers. So SABRC became a rules engine with an evidence agent, and it moved to first priority because UC said it must be in any commercial package.

Sources: Drive: Background for Scoping; Notion: Sep 11 Readout; Strat², 15 Oct 2026; Notion weekly reports, 26–30 Oct and 2–6 Nov 2026; Linear.

### Chapter 5: Design as a selling tool

No designer worked on this account. What stands out is how much designed material a strategist and an engineer produced, and how it moved the deal.

It began with a complaint. In September Vitor said the squad's apps were too dense, and the others agreed. Jaime went looking for tools. He connected Mobbin, a library of screenshots from well-made apps, to Claude Code and Figma. He would describe a screen, pick reference apps, and get wireframes and then a final design. The first full prototype took under an hour and a half. The Command Center's dashboard, ask and search screens borrow from Midday, Sana AI and Dropbox Dash.

The prototype was built to feel real: a drawer on the right of a supplier page, a Verify button, three agents visibly at work, the evidence behind each answer. A narrated video, made in about 40 minutes with Claude Code, Remotion and a synthetic narrator, opened the pitch. The proposal itself was an interactive page where Todd could slide between price and delivery time.

Each piece had a job. The video gave Todd words for investors. The prototype let UC see the punch-out gap closed. The slider turned a price shock into a choice. The lesson the strat team took from it is to make prototypes as real as possible and to use the client's own data.

Sources: Squad Lab, 10 Sep 2026; Squad Lab, 29 Sep 2026; Squad Lab, 6 Oct 2026; Strat², 15 Oct 2026; Strat², 12 Nov 2026.

### Chapter 6: How delivery has gone

Phase 1 went to plan. The handoff from the departing developer finished in July. Six certifiers were live by the end of July and eight by the end of August. The API, its documentation and access controls were ready on 4 Sep. LabViva ran a first test in September. Todd's feedback each week was warm, and weekly commitments were mostly met.

Three things were harder. SABRC turned out to have no certifier behind it. The honest finding, given to Todd on 11 Sep, was that four data sources would yield about 1,500 to 2,000 products. The catalog reached 631, and the largest source, UL SPOT, still blocks access. The blueprint milestone was due 2 Oct and stands at 72%.

The second was dependence on others. The pilot needs LabViva's interface and JAGGAER's services team, and both moved slowly. The third was housekeeping: work started in the client's repository, two Railway projects existed where one is the rule, and a Railway incident on 21 Oct forced key rotation, including a Green Seal key only the client could reissue.

The squad runs the account in weekly cycles with Linear as the record. Harvy gives it about a quarter of his time. Since early November no new build has started, by decision, until the expansion is signed.

Sources: Linear; Notion: Sep 11 Readout; Notion weekly reports; Slack #acnt-ethic-select-internal; Weekly Review + Next Cycle Plan, 23 Oct and 6 Nov 2026.

Todd Capps is the whole client. He is founder, sponsor, buyer and daily contact, and he is rated Champion with high influence. He is also thin on resources: no team to run a new product line, and about $150,000 to spend. Zohar Ziv, the board chairman, advises him on structure.

On Supernal's side the cast changed often. Jack Parham sold the deal and Thomas wrote the proposal. Jaime took over strategy in July and Harvy has been the engineer throughout. Johnny entered in late August and owns the commercial conversation; Stu became account manager in mid-September. The handoffs left a gap: nobody on the delivery side knew exactly what Todd had been led to expect, which is why Thomas was brought back for the 9 Oct call.

Johnny and Jaime disagree on one thing that matters: whether Todd should see hours and headcount. Johnny says he needs them for investors. Jaime wants outcomes only.

### Chapter 8: Where it stands and what is next

As of 13 Nov 2026 the account is healthy in relationship and fragile in money. UC has agreed in principle to a contract with Ethic Select. Todd sent his commercialization proposal to Vickie, Paul and Abim on 9 Nov. Supernal owes him resource and cost estimates for two roadmaps: finish by the end of February, or by the end of March. Linear already holds the second: Command Center by 29 Jan 2027, extension by 26 Feb, SABRC Agent by 31 Mar.

Johnny plans to ask Todd to sign the expansion when they meet on the weekend of 14–15 Nov, possibly with billing delayed until UC pays. The expected close is 20 Nov. The company goal is to close Phase 2 by 31 Dec.

Several risks are open. The client has funds for year one only, so renewal in June 2027 depends on Ethic Select earning or raising money. The month-6 exit window opens around now. UC's browser is Edge and installs may need IT rights, so adoption by requisitioners is unproven. The legal questions from September, who is liable for an agent's determination and whether the platform agreements restrict a direct buyer surface, have no owner. And the team has never agreed with UC what good looks like in numbers.

The next proof point is a simulation on UC's own purchasing data, once Vickie shares it.
