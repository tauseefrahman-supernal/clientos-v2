## 4. Why they bought

**The first deal (Feb 2025).** Not found in this version. The reason is implied by the product: the book took 150 to 200 hours per client by hand (Webinar re: Heritage Vault, 30 Sep 2026; inferred as the original motive).

**Phase 2 (Expansion, Apr 2026).** Several causes together:
- A 28-page vision document showed the transformation potential after a mid-engagement roadblock; the client began resourcing Phase 2 straight after (All Hands, 28 Apr 2026).
- The goal changed from an internal tool to a product Heritage can license to other advisers (Catch Up: Sven & Tauseef, 28 Aug 2026).
- The client committed to becoming an "AI-native company powered by Supernal" (All Hands, 28 Apr 2026).
- A presentation call was described by Tauseef as transformative (AI strategist role reflections, 9 Apr 2026, match excerpt only).

**Acceleration (Jun 2026).** Heritage raised $2M to $3M and wanted three times the pace (Catch Up: Sven & Tauseef, 28 Aug 2026). Supernal proposed the accelerated three-month roadmap proactively, and the client said it needed Supernal to plan it (All Hands, 16 Jun 2026). A public launch date of 1 Jan 2027 set the deadline (Heritage + Supernal Weekly Call, 9 Jul 2026).

**Dedicated Private Environment.** Bought as a sales tool to remove security objections, especially from broker-dealers, "even if not strictly required" (Heritage, Ridgeback, Supernal Weekly Call, 17 Aug 2026), and to match Wealth.com's security (same series, 10 Aug 2026). Heritage owns the data and accounts and can take them on exit (17 Aug 2026).

**Videos and websites.** Keeping the work with the team that knows the product story (Heritage + Supernal Weekly Call, 9 Jul 2026). The existing site is a 25-year-old WordPress site (All-Hands, 10 Nov 2026).

**Foreman.** To free Tom Sr. and Tom Jr. from office management for client work, and to enforce follow-up through daily task emails and escalation to Jess (Foreman Review, 21 Jul 2026).

**Insurance CRM.** Smart Office is failing: mass reassignment deleted tasks twice, everything is manual, data sits in five places (Supernal <> Heritage Insurance, 27 Oct 2026).

**Alternatives.** Wealth.com, Vanilla and Luminary are the named competitors (Webinar re: Heritage Vault, 30 Sep 2026). Wealth.com quoted Heritage about $25K a year for a comparable setup (Heritage Platform Weekly Sync, 26 Aug 2026). Heritage was explicitly comparing Supernal with Wealth.com on security (All-Hands, 4 Aug 2026, match excerpt only). Heritage's chosen position is the visual output for existing documents, not document creation (Heritage, Ridgeback, Supernal Weekly Call, 24 Aug 2026).

### Feature rationale

1. **Three human quality-control checkpoints** (document routing, AI answers, mapping to sheets). Reason: human QC is the most effective path to the 99.9% accuracy goal; more AI layers add little (Heritage + Supernal Weekly Call, 9 Jul 2026).
2. **Recommendations live in their own tab**, not in the sheets. Reason: avoid clutter; each links to the relevant section (Heritage Platform Weekly Sync, 13 Aug 2026).
3. **White-labelling is logo and colour only**, with "Powered by Heritage AI" fixed. Reason: brand the output without customising the platform (same).
4. **One concurrent session per seat plus 2FA.** Reason: stop seat sharing under per-seat pricing (same).
5. **Firm-wide feature toggles, not per client.** Reason: per-client control was judged unnecessary; 2 points instead of 4 (Jr, Jens, Chris, 3 Nov 2026).
6. **Each pilot firm gets its own portal.** Reason: no client transfer needed when a firm takes a full licence (same).
7. **No ChatGPT in the chat; Claude and Gemini only.** Reason: no sufficiently secure model available (Heritage Platform Weekly Sync, 21 Oct 2026).
8. **Firms write their own disclosures from a blank slate.** Reason: Chris does not want a platform-supplied template (same).
9. **"Client Ready" toggle replaces the status dropdown**, with automatic "In Progress". Reason: fewer manual steps and no pop-ups (Heritage Platform Weekly Sync, 28 Oct 2026).
10. **Billing and licensing features (#38, #40) deferred to December.** Reason: pricing is not final and building now risks rework (Heritage Platform Weekly Sync, 15 Oct 2026).

### Identifiers and upload security (checked 14 Nov against meetings)

- **Identifiers in the Vault.** Because the Vault holds highly sensitive client data, identifiers (Social Security, passport, tax ID, account numbers) can be shown in full, masked to the last 4 digits, or fully hidden, set per view (Heritage Platform Weekly Sync, 1 Oct 2026). ### 4. The product and why it is built this way

The Vault has five sections: a data sheet, an asset sheet, an insurance schedule, a flow diagram of the estate documents, and an estate distribution chart. Advisers upload documents to a data room. The system labels and routes them, answers a fixed question set for each document type, and maps the answers onto the sheets. Humans check at all three steps, because the team judged human QC the fastest route to the 99.9% accuracy the brand needs. The flow diagram is the differentiator: it colours trust language to show what is fine, what should go and what to use instead. Heritage chose to compete on that visual output and not on drafting documents, where Wealth.com is strong. The licensing goal shapes the rest. Firms are fully separated. White-labelling stops at logo and colour. Seats allow one session at a time. Firms write their own disclosures. Billing features wait until pricing is settled. One weakness surfaced in October: the model learned on documents from top law firms and misreads poorly drafted trusts, which is what licensees will bring.
