## File: Concept-Alignment

## 01 · The Problem
Benjamin and his team listen to songs by hand. They review 300 to 400 songs a week in a Tuesday session, and he wants to reach 300,000 (Supernal Follow Up, 27 Aug 2026; Strat ², 12 Nov 2026). Existing tools such as Chartmetric and his Instagram tastemaker tracker show momentum (virality, playlist adds) but not the song's quality, which he calls its "DNA". Nothing scores a song against his own rubric (Supernal Follow Up, 27 Aug 2026; Strat ², 12 Nov 2026). He is stuck in day-to-day operations and wants to spend more time as a creative force (Supernal Follow Up, 27 Aug 2026).

## 02 · Proposed Approach
Build the Brill Building OS. This is his own platform with an AI employee that:
- scores every song with the Brill Score, a rubric taken from his published articles and course, and explains each score;
- opens a branded Client Portal where his writers and artists upload songs and get coaching;
- from Month 3, scouts artists. When two or more of his tastemakers converge on the same artist, the AI employee flags a match and adds it to a morning briefing and a "Brill Daily" Spotify playlist.

(Drive: Brill Building - Order Form - OF-BRILL-001 - DRAFT v1 (Nov 2); Strat ², 12 Nov 2026)

- An objective read on every song within the first hour of a session (Drive: OF-BRILL-001).
- His judgement reaches writers before the session, through the portal (Drive: OF-BRILL-001).

Brill Building Agent is a new-logo deal with a boutique music publisher. The founder, Benjamin Groff, wants AI to do what his ears do every Tuesday. ### 2. The client and their problem
Benjamin has run music publishing for 30+ years and trained as a songwriter at Berklee. He built his company alongside a publisher that became a global top five, and kept his own firm boutique: 5 staff and 24 writers and artists. His first number one was Beyoncé's "Break My Soul". His goal is 10 songs on the charts at any time, and growth has flattened. The constraint is his ear. He and his team review 300 to 400 songs a week by hand and he wants to reach 300,000. Tools like Chartmetric and his own 200-account Instagram tastemaker tracker show buzz, not quality. He wants to judge a song's intrinsic "DNA" and find fringe outliers before the market does. He also worries that AI is making music sound the same. In discovery he named two agents: a Pitch Agent that matches songs to artists and drafts emails, and a Talent Scout Agent, which he saw as the harder problem and was open to partnering on. Supernal scoped the second one.
Sources: Supernal Follow Up, 27 Aug 2026; Strat ², 12 Nov 2026.

### 3. Why now and why us
Benjamin is already an AI builder. He uses Claude Code and Replit, has a statement bot that cut 3 days of work to 15 minutes, and invests in Suno. He does not need convincing that AI helps. He needed proof it could hear what he hears. Supernal gave him that faster than he expected. Within a week of the first call Belu had a working OS scoring real songs on four rubric parameters taken from his own published articles. At the 10 Sep demo he found it compelling but asked whether it actually worked. On 5 Oct the team ran his own songs blind: one scored 95, one 66, one 56, matching his own view, and he immediately asked for the next meeting. He asked for a Client Portal on a Tuesday and saw it that Friday, and on 30 Oct he said he loved where it was heading. Supernal's edge is speed on the client's own data and a privacy stance he trusts: scoring runs locally, and later model calls sit behind a zero-retention endpoint. The quiet post-Thanksgiving month also gives him a reason to start now.
Sources: Supernal Follow Up, 27 Aug 2026; Strat ², 12 Nov 2026; Slack #prospect-brill, 30 Oct 2026; Deal Desk Weekly Review, 3 Nov 2026.

### 4. What we are proposing and why it is shaped that way
The proposal is a single platform, the Brill Building OS, with one AI employee. Phase 1 (Months 1 to 2) puts Brill Score v1 live within two weeks and finishes the full ten-parameter rubric by Month 2. It also opens a branded Client Portal to five pilot writers. Phase 2 (Months 3 to 5) adds scouting: an artist becomes a match when two or more of his tastemakers converge on them. It also adds a Brill Daily Spotify playlist, SoundCloud signal and an Artist Score, each after a one-page research note on that source. Phase 3 (Months 6 to 12) is a backlog re-ranked with him each cycle. The shape comes from the "2+12" idea agreed on 14 Oct. Two months prove the score works, then roll straight into twelve, which eases his fear of paying for something unproven. The order form makes that real with a termination-for-cause right at the Month 2 checkpoint. He owns the code and the rubric, and the $3,000 Private AI line shows what privacy costs, because protecting unreleased songs was his condition for sharing audio.
