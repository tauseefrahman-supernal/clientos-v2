## 01 · The Problem

- viaPeople has run Waterfall's performance cycles since 2018. It has become unreliable after vendor turnover and a 2026 platform migration (Supernal <> Waterfall Asset Management, 28 Oct 2026).

- Sell it as an ongoing SaaS partnership rather than a one-off build, with Supernal as Waterfall's AI partner (Waterfall Prototype Walkthrough, 12 Nov 2026).
- Plan V1 and V2. V1 covers the core cycles. V2 adds the HRIS census sync, then the ATS (JazzHR) and the comp system (Supernal / Waterfall - Process Discussion, 3 Nov 2026; Supernal <> Waterfall Asset Management, 28 Oct 2026).

## File: Prototype-Summary

## 1. What we're building (one line)
A performance management OS that Waterfall owns, covering goals, check-ins, 360s, year-end reviews and promotions, with AI coaching and an HR assistant.

## 2. Why it matters / sales impact
The prototype showed Alyssa and Sam that Supernal could do what they need, and they asked Supernal to stop building and write the proposal (Waterfall Prototype Walkthrough, 12 Nov 2026). It answered the main objection, that off-the-shelf tools are too rigid, by showing a form editor HR can use itself.

## 3. Target user + core scenario
Alyssa and Sam as HR admins running a cycle, plus managers and employees setting goals and completing reviews (Waterfall Prototype Walkthrough, 12 Nov 2026).

## 4. The core flow
1. HR launches a cycle and the dashboard shows who is on track or behind.
2. HR sends nudges with one click, and the assistant delivers them in chat.
3. An employee drafts a goal, and "Check with Coach" scores it against SMART criteria and suggests links to company goals.
4. The manager approves the goal, and it rolls up to the leader's dashboard.
5. HR edits forms, including HR-only fields (Waterfall Prototype Walkthrough, 12 Nov 2026).

## 5. Data + systems
The demo used sample employees and goals, including a leader's goal about growing private asset-backed credit (Waterfall Prototype Walkthrough, 12 Nov 2026). The nudges were shown in Slack. ## 7. Success criteria

### 2. The client and their problem

Waterfall has about 150 staff, most in New York and just over 20 in London and Dublin. The firm is collaborative, and its teams work across groups. HR is effectively two people, Alyssa and Sam, who run four cycles a year: an April goal check-in, an August 360 for everyone, and year-end reviews plus promotions in November and December. viaPeople used to be a strength because it was customisable and came with advice. After vendor turnover and a 2026 platform migration, which Waterfall volunteered for early, it became unreliable. Promotion forms went to the candidates themselves, and confidential performance and potential ratings were visible to employees. A minor form text change takes 7 to 10 emails to support. Removing a 360 reviewer takes about 10 minutes and triggers wrong notifications. Sam exports data to Excel and pivot tables to see who has finished. The advancement grid, which shows every employee by title and team with experience and date of last promotion, is still a printed 11x17 sheet. Census data is entered twice because nothing connects to the HRIS. Alyssa's quick win is simple: the same cycles, on a system she trusts.
Sources: Supernal <> Waterfall Asset Management, 28 Oct 2026; Supernal / Waterfall - Process Discussion, 3 Nov 2026; Waterfall Prototype Walkthrough, 12 Nov 2026.

### 4. What we are proposing and why it is shaped that way

The offer is an owned performance OS, rebuilt cycle for cycle with what Waterfall lacks today. The prototype showed an HR dashboard with on-track and behind status, one-click nudges and an HR action list. It showed cascading goals that suggest links to leaders' goals, private goals, and weighting sliders tied to bonus thinking. A "Check with Coach" scores goals against SMART criteria. A form editor lets HR change fields and order and hide sensitive fields from employees. The 360 module tracks progress, and partner 360s move out of Word documents. An HR assistant sends nudges through chat and answers process questions; it was shown in Slack, but Waterfall uses Teams. The deal is shaped as a subscription partnership rather than a fixed build, for two reasons. Alyssa values the ongoing advice viaPeople once gave. And continuous iteration is the main justification for charging more than $105K. Integrations come later, because Alyssa said the system can stand alone at first: the HRIS census sync first, then JazzHR and the comp system. Compensation data stays out of scope.
