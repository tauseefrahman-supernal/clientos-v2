## Voice in one sentence

Supernal sounds like a master craftsman who has been building things for thirty years, talking to a successful business owner about what they're going to build next together. Plainspoken, confident in the work, never claiming the expertise.

---

## Five voice attributes

### 1. Crafted

- **Definition:** Specific, named, detailed. Every claim points at a real object, person, or moment. The work shows; we don't have to announce it.
- **Do:** "We spent three weeks tuning the agent that handles their intake. It now reads handwritten work orders that confused the last vendor's OCR for two years."
- **Don't:** "We deliver high-quality, custom-built AI solutions tailored to each client's unique needs."

### 2. Plainspoken

- **Definition:** Words a smart operator uses over coffee. No jargon, no consulting register, no AI mystique. The vocabulary is accessible; the ideas are not.
- **Do:** "Most AI tools today look great in a demo and fall over in a real workflow. We build the version that doesn't fall over."
- **Don't:** "We leverage cutting-edge AI infrastructure to drive transformative business outcomes through bespoke implementations."

### 3. In the doing, not the claiming

- **Definition:** Swagger comes from describing the work, not from asserting authority. Show what was built, who used it, what changed. Never say "we are experts."
- **Do:** "Last month we shipped an agent that handles a 40-person logistics team's dispatch. It books 60% of jobs without a human touching it."
- **Don't:** "We are leading experts in AI agent development for logistics companies."

### 4. Permanent

- **Definition:** Word choices that could have been written in 1995 and will read fine in 2035. No phrases tied to this quarter's hype. The voice has weight and age.
- **Do:** "What we build should still be running ten years from now. That changes how you build it."
- **Don't:** "In today's rapidly evolving AI landscape, next-gen solutions are reshaping the future of work."

### 5. Companionable

- **Definition:** We talk *with* the reader, not *at* them. The reader is a peer, often someone who has run a real business longer than we have. We respect their work. We're partners, not preachers.
- **Do:** "You already know your business better than any consultant ever will. Our job is to figure out where AI fits, with you."
- **Don't:** "Let us guide you through your AI transformation journey."

---

## What we don't sound like

1. **Not a consulting firm.** No "transformation," no "strategic imperatives," no $8M three-year roadmaps. Scott's anti-reference: "we'll walk down the road and find the smarter crossing."
2. **Not an AI hype account.** No "this changes everything," no "the future of work," no breathless takes on what AI will do. We describe what it's already doing for one specific customer.
3. **Not a thought-leader.** No frameworks numbered 1-5, no "lessons learned," no LinkedIn-influencer sermon structure. We tell stories about builds.

## Vocabulary

### Words and phrases we use

build, ship, craft, partner, sherpa, ready, AI ready, AI curious, give your team superpowers, the work, the team, customer, real, actually, today, this week, here's how, what we built, what we learned, the operator, the business, the workflow, in practice, in the wild, when it works, when it doesn't

### Words and phrases we ban

- AI infrastructure (alone, without saying what it unlocks)
- replace human hires
- digital transformation
- data modernization
- transformation journey
- leverage (as a verb)
- synergy
- ecosystem
- next-gen
- cutting-edge
- revolutionize
- game-changer
- world-class
- best-in-class
- robust
- scalable solution
- seamless
- empower
- unlock potential
- in today's fast-paced world
- thrilled to announce
- excited to share

### Why the top 5 bans exist

- **Digital transformation / data modernization** — Palantir and McKinsey vocabulary. Signals consulting, which we are not.
- **Replace human hires** — wrong frame entirely. We give teams superpowers; we don't replace them. This is a positioning rule, enforced at the word level.
- **Cutting-edge / next-gen** — phrases with a 2-year shelf life. The voice has to read well in ten years.
- **Leverage (verb)** — corporate filler that means nothing. Use the verb that says what's actually happening.

## Sentence and paragraph rules

- **Sentence length:** mix short and medium. Most sentences 6-18 words. A 3-word sentence is allowed and often the strongest beat. Sentences over 25 words must be load-bearing.
- **Paragraph length on LinkedIn:** maximum 3 lines per paragraph. Most are 1-2.
- **Paragraph length in long-form (YouTube scripts, articles):** maximum 4 lines per paragraph.
- **Em dashes:** banned. Use a period.
- **Exclamation points:** banned in default copy. Allowed only in genuine surprise (rare).
- **Semicolons:** avoid. Break the sentence.
- **Ellipses:** only for trailing thought. Never as a stylistic tic.
- **First person:** "we" is the default voice when speaking as Supernal. "I" is allowed for Scott's personal posts and for first-person customer stories. Never mix "we" and "I" in the same piece without a reason.
- **Tense:** present and past tense for describing work. Avoid future tense ("we will," "we're going to") except when naming a specific upcoming ship.
- **Numbers:** prefer specific numbers over rounded ones. "47 hours a week" beats "around 50 hours."

## Sample paragraphs

### IN VOICE — about our work

> We shipped an intake agent for a 22-person home services business last month. It reads work orders, books crews, and texts customers when the team is en route. Before, the owner's wife was running this from a kitchen table. She's back to running the business side, which is what she's actually good at.
>
> The agent isn't doing anything magic. It's doing the boring parts of the job, the parts that were eating four hours a day. That's most of what good agents do. The magic is what happens to the team's calendar afterward.

### IN VOICE — about the customer and market

> Most operators we talk to already know AI is going to matter for their business. That's new. A year ago we were explaining why; today we're explaining how, and which part to do first.
>
> The hard question isn't whether to do this. It's who you trust to walk you through it. There's a lot of bad advice in this market right now, and most of it sounds confident. We try to sound like the people who've actually built the thing.

### OUT OF VOICE → REWRITE 1

❌ **Wrong:**
> "In today's rapidly evolving AI landscape, small and medium businesses face unprecedented opportunities to harness the transformative power of intelligent agents. Our cutting-edge platform empowers organizations to unlock new efficiencies and drive measurable business outcomes through tailored AI solutions."

✅ **In voice:**
> "Most small businesses we work with don't need a platform. They need one workflow handled, well. We start with the workflow that's eating the most hours this week and build the agent that takes it off the team's plate."

**What changed and why:**
- Killed "rapidly evolving AI landscape" (banned phrase, hype, dated).
- Killed "transformative power" and "harness" (consulting register).
- Replaced "empowers organizations" with a concrete subject + verb ("we start with the workflow").
- Replaced "cutting-edge platform" with what we actually do.
- Replaced "tailored AI solutions" with a specific build sequence.

### OUT OF VOICE → REWRITE 2

❌ **Wrong:**
> "We are thrilled to announce that Supernal AI is leading the charge in AI-native infrastructure for forward-thinking SMBs. Our team of expert builders leverages best-in-class technology to deliver bespoke, scalable solutions that empower businesses to thrive in the age of AI."

✅ **In voice:**
> "We build AI agents for small businesses. The kind that ship and stay shipped. This week we crossed 50 customers running agents we built. Most of them found us through other operators, which is the only signal we trust."

**What changed and why:**
- Killed "thrilled to announce" (banned preamble).
- Killed "leading the charge" (claim, not work).
- Killed "AI-native" (banned in customer copy — investor word).
- Killed "expert builders" (claiming the expertise instead of showing the work).
- Killed "leverages," "best-in-class," "bespoke," "scalable solutions," "empower," "thrive," "age of AI" (vocabulary bans, all in one sentence).
- Added a specific number (50 customers) and a specific signal (operator referrals) — concrete proof instead of abstract claims.

## Quality test

Apply these to any draft. If any answer is "no," the draft fails the voice check.

1. **Specificity:** Can I point to at least one specific customer, number, build, or moment in this piece? (If everything is abstract, fail.)
2. **In the doing:** Are we describing work being done, or claiming we're good at the work? (Claiming = fail.)
3. **Permanence:** Could this paragraph have been written in 1995 or read fine in 2035, with the topic adjusted? (Hype-tied phrasing = fail.)
4. **Vocabulary:** Are any banned words present? (Run a search. One banned word = fail.)
5. **Em dash:** Is there a single em dash anywhere? (One = fail.)
6. **Companionable:** Does this read like we're talking *with* a peer, or *at* an audience? (Sermon mode = fail.)

3. **Tone in the "monster" content.** Scott wants the risk narrative dialed up — "there should be a monster stalking people a little bit." The current spec leans steady and grounded. Should there be a separate sub-mode for risk-narrative content that's tighter, sharper, slightly more urgent? Worth a 15-minute conversation.
4. **The English garden / sherpa metaphor in copy.** These are great internal language. Should they appear in customer-facing copy ever, or stay as our internal compass? My recommendation: stay internal. Confirm.
5. **How much can the voice flex by channel?** A LinkedIn post and a YouTube long-form script are different forms. The vocabulary and bans hold across both. Sentence rhythm and pacing differ. Should there be a channel-specific addendum?
6. **First sample of Scott's actual writing.** I drafted the in-voice samples above from his metaphors and positioning. Pull 5-10 sentences Scott has actually written (LinkedIn, internal Slack, podcast transcripts) and stress-test the spec against them. Where they diverge from the spec, the spec adjusts — not Scott.
