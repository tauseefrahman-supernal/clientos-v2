## 3. Brand Positioning

### Core Positioning Statement
Supernal is an infrastructure-first AI transformation partner for operationally mature SMBs. AI employees are built on top of the infrastructure — the infrastructure is the product, not the agents themselves.

### The Key Differentiator
Most AI platforms sell picks and shovels — tools you use to build AI workers yourself. Supernal builds, trains, deploys, and maintains the workers for you. Bespoke. Tailored. End-to-end.

> "The magic is in the customization, not the standardization."
> — Scott Downes, CEO

### Positioning Pillars
- **Closed service** — white-glove, fully managed, not self-serve
- **Surgical builds** — scoped precisely to where AI delivers the highest impact
- **Fast tangible results** — not a 6-month implementation; measurable outcomes quickly
- **Bespoke by design** — custom-trained on client data, tone, and workflows; not off-the-shelf

### Customer Journey Framing
**AI Curious → AI Ready → AI Native**
- "AI Ready" is preferred as customer-facing language ("AI native" skews more investor/tech)
- Supernal is the guide through all three stages, not just the entry point

### The Iron Man Suit Philosophy
The human is always in control. Technology gives them superpowers. This is augmentation, not replacement. Every piece of content should reinforce this — AI as leverage, not threat.

### Internal Metaphor (for team reference)
> "A new door appears in your office. Behind it, a factory of robots building for you."

## 4. Target Customer

### Primary Buyer
Operationally mature, founder-led or legacy SMBs. Positive cash flow. Not existentially threatened yet, but aware AI is coming. Warren Buffett / Munger mentality — disciplined, cash-flow focused, not trend-chasing.

> *"People who set budgets, not follow budgets."*

**Key industries:**
- Trades: HVAC, plumbing, landscaping
- Insurance
- Financial Services
- Healthcare
- SaaS / Technology
- Retail & Logistics
- Professional Services
- Real Estate

### Three Buyer Archetypes

**1. AI Champion (CFO-level)**
Career-advancement focused. Sees AI as a strategic move that makes them look visionary. Wants ROI they can present to a board. Risk-aware but motivated. Messaging angle: *"Make your next big move before someone else does."*

**2. Urgent Technologist**
Digital native. "Oh f***, we should have done this already" mentality. Moves fast. Wants proof of concept quickly. Messaging angle: *"You're not behind. But you will be if you wait."*

**3. Legacy Builder**
60–70 year old wealthy owner transforming the business for the next generation. Wants to preserve institutional knowledge and future-proof operations. Values trust and human relationships over tech jargon. Messaging angle: *"Preserve what you've built. Multiply it."*

### Secondary Buyer: Technology Leader (CTO/IT)
Ranges from defensive ("I'm already your AI guy") to enthusiastic ("thank god you're here"). Content must address this person directly — not just as an objection to handle. They are often the internal champion or the internal obstacle.

## 5. Brand Voice & Tone

### Voice Archetype
**Guide** — not a vendor, not a tech evangelist, not a fear-monger.
Think: doula, Sherpa, Yoda. A trusted expert who has walked this path many times and is here to make the journey easier for the client.

### Hero's Journey Framing
- **The client is the hero** — a successful, experienced leader facing the necessity of AI transformation
- **Supernal is the guide** — experienced, calm, capable, never the center of attention
- **The enemy is complexity and inertia** — not competitors, not humans, not AI itself

### Tone Spectrum
- **80% consultative** — speaking with clients as peers, surfacing insight, asking the right questions
- **20% educational** — sharing context and frameworks only when it serves the client's decision-making

### Voice Qualities
| DO | DON'T |
|---|---|
| Speak as a peer and trusted advisor | Speak down or explain the obvious |
| Acknowledge real AI risks | Fear-monger or overhype |
| Be candid and direct | Be polished or performative |
| Use specific, grounded language | Use vague AI buzzwords without meaning |
| Tell stories behind the work | Lead with features or specs |
| Build energy and momentum | Be boring or corporate |
| Position AI as leverage | Position AI as replacement |

### Scott's Content Philosophy
- **Building in public** — candid, behind-the-scenes content over polished case studies
- **Demo culture** — show the work, not just the concept
- **"Be the most exciting meeting of their week"** — content should feel alive, not like a brochure
- **Abundance mentality** — not prescriptive; supports what works for each client

### Banned Phrases & Patterns
- "Revolutionary" / "game-changing" / "disruptive" (without proof)
- AI as existential threat framing
- Generic productivity claims without numbers
- "Leverage AI to unlock your potential" — empty filler
- Passive, hedge-y language ("might," "could potentially")

## 13. Content Guardrails

1. **The client is always the hero.** Supernal is the guide. Never center the brand over the client's transformation.
2. **No fear-mongering.** Acknowledge AI risks. Target bad actors, not AI itself.
3. **Specificity over hype.** Every claim needs a number or a story behind it.
4. **Consultative, not educational.** Don't explain what AI is. Talk about what it does for this specific business.
5. **Building in public over polish.** Raw, real, behind-the-scenes > slick corporate content.
6. **Iron Man, not Terminator.** Human in control. AI as leverage. Always.
7. **Match the buyer's language.** "AI ready" for customers. "AI native" for investors. Know the room.
8. **Proactive, not reactive framing.** Supernal's AI employees act before being asked. Content should feel that way too — one step ahead.
