# Offering and Commercials

> How Supernal prices and structures work today: the offerings, the internal plans and margin bands behind every price, contract terms, how pods and squads shape cost, and the rules an order form and proposal must follow. Stella uses this when drafting a proposal or order form, sizing an expansion, checking whether a deal meets the margin floor, and answering client pushback. Refreshed 2026-11-13. The pricing doc calls itself a default, not a rule: "the reality of sales at an early-stage startup is case by case".

## Principles

- Sell outcomes, not hours or people. Exceptions exist (Breakr was sold on hours and staffing); honour them and move them toward outcomes.
- Plans and staffing are internal delivery models. Never show them to clients.
- Never quote hours, headcount, points or engineer-for-engineer comparisons in a proposal or order form.
- Push for value creation and outcomes in every conversation, including expansion.
- Staffing changes are reviewed every quarter. A 3-month staffing lock-in can apply but is rare.

## How to build a deal (in order)

1. Understand the lead: problems, needs, buying intent, tier (Mission 200 or not), taxonomy.
2. Pick the offering: AI Transformation Partner, AI Native Platform Partner, or both.
3. Pick the plan: S, M, L, XL or Custom (internal only).
4. Pick what we do from the capability list (below) based on their problems.
5. Check the margin: 40% floor, 45% ideal floor, 60% target, 80% ceiling.
6. Prepare for pushbacks.

## Subscriptions and contract terms

- Subscriptions, not projects. Retainer over project, always.
- 12 months minimum, with renewal at a set rate.
- Opt-out at 6 months, which can come down to 3 if needed.
- Supernal scopes the first 3 months.
- Live by week 2, value by month 1.
- More than one problem at a time, or a move toward a platform, means an expansion or a bigger plan.

## Offerings

Both offerings price between 40% and 60% margin (sales control).

- **AI Transformation Partner (default).** End-to-end AI transformation: data layer, AI OS and AI employees, with a live roadmap.
- **AI Native Platform Partner (bespoke).** A new product or revenue line, or an extension of the Transformation Partner. Starts around 1.5 masons + ⅓ strat (not final).
- **Diagnostic.** About $5,375 at about 80% margin. 2-week sprint, one-off, no renewal. About 10 hours strat, about 5 client calls, about 5 hours mason. Should lead into a Transformation or Platform deal.
- **AI training and workshops.** To be decided.

See Product-Suite.md for which clients sit on which offering.

## Pods, squads and leverage

- **Pod.** What the client buys in practice: 1 strat, 1 mason, 1 AM, 1 account at minimum. A pod grows by account expansion, usually +1 or +2 masons. Soft ceiling around 6 people, then split. "Adding a pod" to an account is a Q4 expansion target (4 accounts by Dec 31).
- **Squad.** The internal delivery group: strats and masons who stay together across several accounts. Minimum 2 strats and 2 masons. One squad ran in pilot (Diana, Jaime, Vitor, Harvey on Ethic Select, Hooray, Super73, Labyrinth); a second squad is being onboarded; the goal is 3 squads by Dec 31.
- **Leverage** = revenue managed / total comp. The north-star number for pods and squads. Worked example (Sep 2026): pilot squad managed $444K against $379K cost, 1.17x, target 1.5x.
- **Strat load target:** 3 to 4 accounts or $400K to $1M managed revenue per strat.
- **Capacity points** (Capacity Steering): Mason Full 1.0, Partial 0.5; Strat Heavy 0.5, Medium 0.25, Light 0.1. Around 1.0 per person is fully loaded. Staffing a deal at plan M (1 mason + ⅓ strat) maps roughly to Full + Medium (inferred).

"Points" in the brief: Notion uses load points for capacity only. No current Notion page prices work in points, and the proposal checklist forbids points in client documents.

## Order form rules

The MSA owns the relationship. The order form owns the deal.

| MSA | Order form |
|---|---|
| Governing law and jurisdiction | Parties and OF number |
| Liability cap (for example 12 months of fees) | Scope of services |
| IP ownership framework | Deliverables and milestones |
| Confidentiality | Fees and payment schedule |
| Indemnification | Term and exit rights |
| Data protection principles | Named contacts |
| Termination for cause | Client obligations |
| Independent contractor status | Third-party cost handling |
| Insurance | Change control for this project |
| Document precedence (OF over MSA) | Auto-renewal or path to the next OF |

Same OF or new OF:
- Priorities shift inside the same problem space: keep the same OF.
- Genuinely new product area, functional area or entity: new OF.
- High-value client, pod repurposed to create value faster: no new OF; manage risk through account reviews.

Scope boundary: state the specific problem goal, and name what sits outside it. On smaller accounts, if you cannot name three things explicitly out of scope, the boundary is too wide.

Milestones:
- 4 to 6 milestones over 3 months, sequenced as a client journey.
- Binary, verifiable by someone outside the team in 30 seconds, a new capability or state, value without the how.
- Milestone 1 includes systems discovery, integration strategy and KPI definition.
- Something live by week 2, without promising full integration.
- The final onboarding milestone (about month 3) closes against the Milestone 1 KPIs.
- Plain, value-framed language ("Sales team can ask any question about an account and get a sourced answer", not "Implement RAG pipeline").

Before sending (checklist):
- OF number assigned.
- Problem goal specific; out-of-scope named.
- 4 to 6 binary milestones; Milestone 1 covers systems and KPIs; live by week 2.
- Run the Order Form Review Checklist.

## Proposal and order form review checklist

- **Deal classification:** industry, business type, department impacted, deal type, use cases, 2 to 3 metrics Supernal is accountable for, why it matters now.
- **Milestones:** month 1 working output (binary); month 3 end state of the pilot or phase.
- **AI strategy doc timing:** 3-month pilot, drafted month 2 and presented end of month 2; 12-month transformation, shared around end of month 1.
- **Gates:** Revenue (margin viable, can be staffed), Strat (right problem, sequence, timelines), Build (right solution and tool), Staffing (named people, kickoff within 2 weeks).
- **Sign-offs:** Legal and commercial, Ryan and Johnny. Strat and technical, Seb and Thomas. Build, FDEs plus Naveen and Thomas. Staffing, Seb and Naveen.
- **Scope:** why, who uses it and when, who gives feedback and who approves, dependencies, sync rhythm (weekly, monthly milestone review, quarterly QBR with renewal), escalation path.
- **Tools and infrastructure:** tools in client accounts (client pays directly), or estimated costs with a monthly ceiling and anything above paid by the client.
- **IP and code ownership:** one of: client owns code and infra (clean exit); client owns code, Supernal owns DB/infra (migration fee to exit); client uses Supernal proprietary tech (Supernal owns API and platform).
- **Do not:** use em dashes; put hours or points in the OF or proposal; use phases when v1 is clear; write open-ended deliverables ("improve", "support", "assist"); write a 12-month scope without clear month 1 and month 3 milestones; send without all sign-offs.

## Scoping questions before pricing

Lead (problems, needs, intent, tier), speed (time to live, reporting cadence), scope (one problem or many, one layer or the full stack), security (private LLMs, data sovereignty, Iron Curtain), training (in or out), legal (in or out), contract (length, renewal rate, opt-out), support (SLA, escalation), exit (ownership and portability).

## Pushback answers (from the pricing doc)

- "We'll hire ourselves." In-house equivalent is about $860K a year in salaries (fractional CTO about $180K, 2 engineers about $147K each, designer about $145K, PM about $140K, QA about $103K), over $1M with benefits, plus 3 to 6 months to hire. Plan M is about $190K to $290K a year. If they hire, that person works with us.
- "We need a data lake first." The data layer is step one of what we deliver, built in weeks with a first use case on top.
- "Can we pay for hours?" No. Embedded people, priced on outcomes.
- "Offshore is cheaper." It needs management, direction and feedback, and often does not get there.
- "Small pilot first." 12-month subscription with a 6-month opt-out, reducible to 3. Live in 2 weeks, value in month 1.
- "We already have ChatGPT, Copilot or Claude." Having tools is not being AI native. We drive adoption at individual, department and company level, track it, then move data and apps into an operating system with AI employees on top.
- "Lock-in or IP?" The client owns the data and the code. Value compounds, the price does not.
